# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Indian women in their 20s–40s, shopping for skincare that works for Indian skin. They are health-conscious and research-aware — tired of brands that overpromise or use ingredients that cause breakouts. When they land on a product page, their first question is not "does this look nice?" but "is this actually safe for me?" They want to feel seen, not sold to.

Secondary audience: gift-buyers and partners who want something thoughtful and trustworthy for the women in their lives.

## Product Purpose

PureAstra is a D2C skincare brand selling plant-based, cruelty-free, toxin-conscious formulations developed specifically for Indian skin types. The storefront is the primary channel — it must earn trust before it earns a transaction.

Success looks like a customer who reads the ingredient story, believes it, adds to cart, and comes back because the formula actually delivered.

## Brand Personality

Calm. Clean. Trustworthy.

PureAstra speaks with quiet authority — like a knowledgeable friend who genuinely cares, not a brand rep closing a sale. Warmth is earned through transparency (founder intent, ingredient sourcing, formulation rationale), not injected through decorative warmth or cozy visuals. The tone is direct, reassuring, and human.

## Anti-references

- **Loud D2C brands** (Mamaearth, WOW, generic Indian beauty ads): No aggressive promotion, neon sale banners, cluttered "bestseller" grids, or urgency-manufactured UX. No screaming.
- **Cold luxury skincare** (La Mer, Sulwhasoo, Dior Beauty): No aloofness, intimidating minimalism, or premium distance. Should feel approachable, not aspirational in a way that excludes.
- **Generic Ayurvedic aesthetic**: No rustic earthy-brown overload, temple imagery, "ancient wisdom" framing, or herb-and-mortar photography as the default visual language.
- **Corporate wellness sterility**: No soul-free clinical whitespace, generic sans-serif uniformity, or anything that strips the brand of human personality.

## Design Principles

1. **Reassurance over persuasion.** Every touch-point answers "is this right for my skin?" before it asks for a click. Trust is the conversion event.
2. **Earned warmth.** Brand personality lives in copy, imagery, and founder context — not in a cream background. Color warmth used as shorthand for "natural" is a tell; avoid it.
3. **Indian skin, not adapted global.** Formulated for Indian skin from the start — the design should reflect that specificity, not a Western beauty aesthetic with Indian text dropped in.
4. **Transparency as design element.** Ingredient lists, formulation stories, and production philosophy are visual hierarchy, not footnotes. Show your work.
5. **Quiet confidence.** The best skincare brands don't need urgency mechanics or decorative complexity to convert. Restraint is the aesthetic.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA minimum. All body text ≥ 4.5:1 contrast; large/bold headings ≥ 3:1. Placeholder text must hit 4.5:1 — not muted gray defaults.

Imagery features a range of Indian skin tones; UI must remain readable at every background combination those photos introduce. Full `prefers-reduced-motion` support — the site already lazy-loads video; all animations need the reduced-motion alternative wired correctly.
